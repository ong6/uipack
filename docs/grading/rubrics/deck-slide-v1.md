# Deck rubric, version 1

Frozen on 2026-11-06. A change to any line is version 2 in a new file; this file never changes.
Score each deck 1 to 10 per criterion against the descriptions of 3, 6 and 9, citing a slide and a
region (or quoted words) for every score. A severe flaw caps a criterion at 4. The overall score is
your judgement, not an average. Use the same rubric for the reference deck.

| Score | Means |
| --- | --- |
| 9 | A top consultant or staff engineer would present it unchanged |
| 6 | The argument is there, but the room would ask what a slide means |
| 3 | The audience could not act on it |

## Criteria

### S1 Titles tell the argument

Definition: read in order, the slide titles alone carry the argument; each is a claim or a spoken
verdict, not a topic label.

- 3: Topic titles ("Results", "Next steps"); the argument lives only in the notes.
- 6: Most titles are claims; two or three are labels, or the order wanders.
- 9: The titles alone are a coherent summary, answer first.

Check: write the titles as a list. Does it read as the argument? Mark each title that is a label.

### S2 One idea per slide

Definition: each slide makes one point, with words, a figure and whitespace that serve it.

- 3: Slides with several points, walls of text, or a figure unrelated to the title.
- 6: Mostly one idea; some slides crowded or carrying a second point.
- 9: Every slide has one point and one visual that proves it, with room around both.

Check: for each slide, state its one point in under ten words; flag slides needing two.

### S3 Evidence bound to claims

Definition: numbers carry what they measure, a window, a comparison point and a source; weak
evidence is visible, not buried.

- 3: Bare numbers, unsourced claims, or costs and risks missing.
- 6: Most numbers sourced; a comparison point or a window missing, or a risk understated.
- 9: Every number has unit, window, comparison and source; trade-offs sit next to the claims they qualify.

Check: list every number; mark each missing its unit, window, comparison point or source.

### S4 Slide craft

Definition: layout, legibility, hierarchy and consistency across slides; nothing clipped or crowded.

- 3: Clipped or overlapping text, inconsistent layouts, unreadable labels.
- 6: Clean, but templated, with uneven alignment or chart labels that need squinting.
- 9: A consistent system; every label readable from the back of the room; charts labelled on the data.

Check: find the smallest text in the deck and the slide whose layout differs from the system.

### S5 The ask

Definition: the deck ends with a concrete decision or action, an owner and a date.

- 3: No ask, or "questions?".
- 6: An ask without an owner or a date, or several asks.
- 9: One clear decision with owner, date and what happens if it is not taken.

Check: quote the ask; name its owner and date.
