# Landing page rubric, version 1

Frozen on 2026-11-06. A change to any line is version 2 in a new file; this file never changes.
Score each page 1 to 10 per criterion against the descriptions of 3, 6 and 9, citing a screenshot
and a region (or quoted words) for every score. A severe flaw caps a criterion at 4. The overall
score is your judgement, not an average. Use the same rubric for the reference page.

| Score | Means |
| --- | --- |
| 9 | A top professional team would ship it unchanged |
| 6 | Competent and clear, but looks assembled from defaults; a designer would send it back once |
| 3 | Broken, confusing or placeholder in places a visitor would notice in the first minute |

## Criteria

### L1 Message in five seconds

Definition: from the first screen alone, a visitor can say what the product is, who it is for and
what to do next.

- 3: The headline is a slogan or a category word; the action is missing or one of several equal buttons.
- 6: The headline names the product and the outcome; one primary action, but the subline repeats the headline or hides the audience.
- 9: Headline, subline and primary action read as one sentence of intent; nothing above the fold competes with them.

Check: cover everything below the first 900px of the 1440 screenshot. Write the product, the
audience and the next step in one line each. A line you cannot write scores 5 or less.

### L2 The product shown, not described

Definition: the largest visual shows the product's real output with specific data, not an
abstract illustration, stock icons or an empty frame.

- 3: The hero visual is empty, decorative, a stock icon flow, or captured mid-animation.
- 6: A real product surface, but generic data (lorem, "Item 1") or too small to read.
- 9: A filled, legible product surface with specific numbers and nouns from the brief that agree with the copy.

Check: name three specific values (numbers, names, dates) readable in the hero visual at 1440.
Fewer than three scores 5 or less; an empty or blank region over a third of the visual scores 3.

### L3 Typography and hierarchy

Definition: type sizes, weights and spacing make a clear reading order; a small number of sizes
used consistently.

- 3: Several competing sizes, cramped or uneven line lengths, headings that do not stand apart.
- 6: A sound scale; a few places where sizes or weights drift, or the body measure runs long.
- 9: One deliberate scale; every section reads in the same order; body lines sit at 45 to 80 characters.

Check: count distinct heading sizes in the 1440 screenshot (more than five is a flag) and estimate
the longest body line's characters.

### L4 Layout and alignment craft

Definition: a consistent grid, equal spacing between equal things, and boxes that line up.

- 3: Misaligned edges, cards of different heights in one row, spacing that changes section to section.
- 6: A grid is visible; one or two rows where heights, gaps or edges break it.
- 9: Every edge and gap belongs to the grid; cards in a row share a height and their actions line up.

Check: for each row of cards or paired boxes, compare the bottom edges. Any visible mismatch scores
6 or less.

### L5 Identity

Definition: the page has a recognisable, intentional character that suits the product, rather than
a template look.

- 3: Interchangeable with a starter template; default palette, default illustration style.
- 6: Some owned details (a colour, a motif), but the overall impression is a familiar template.
- 9: A visitor could recognise the brand from a crop of any section; the character serves the product.

Check: crop one section without the logo. Could it belong to five other products? Yes scores 5 or less.

### L6 Robust at 390 and in dark

Definition: the phone and dark views are designed, not squeezed or inverted.

- 3: Overflow, clipped text, unreadable contrast, or a broken layout at 390 or in dark.
- 6: Works, but the phone view is the desktop stacked, or dark mode has muddy surfaces.
- 9: Phone and dark views are as considered as 1440 light; hierarchy and the product visual survive.

Check: compare the four screenshots section by section; list each place the 390 or dark view loses
something the 1440 light view has.
